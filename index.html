Which appropriate station can receive this group, and has the receiving team confirmed?

A narrow coordination add-on for authorised Hajj field teams. It sends a group that needs rest to a cooling or assistance station that has room for the whole group, suits its access needs, is on an operator-approved route, and has confirmed it will receive them.

📱Group coordinatorPhone. Enters an anonymous group size, approximate location and access needs. Sees only stations that fit the whole group, with walking distance and data age.
📋Station staffTablet. Open, pause or close. Tap people in and out, log walk-ins. Accept, decline or “call me” for each request.
🖥SupervisorDesktop. All stations in one view: space, incoming groups, stale data, overrides with recorded reasons.

What has actually been built and tested, and what has not

Every claim below is tagged. Tested means we ran it and it passed. Illustrative means a synthetic simulation, not evidence of real-world benefit. Proposed means a design choice still to be validated. Not yet tested means exactly that.

Tested

Rules engine, two implementations

The allocation rules are in Python (engine/zill_engine.py), and this browser app runs a line-for-line JavaScript port. The 7 scripted scenarios pass in both, and the browser reproduces the Python output character for character.

Built

Three role screens, one live state

Coordinator phone, station tablet and supervisor desktop run on one shared state. Each screen can be opened in its own window and stays in sync. Works offline from a single folder, with no server, account or tracking.

Illustrative

Baseline comparison on synthetic demand

300 simulated runs of 3 hours of synthetic group demand (engine/simulate.py). Useful for spotting failure modes, not for claiming benefit.

Not yet tested

Real people, real places

No interviews, operator partner, pilot site or real data exist yet. The time limits (3, 15 and 20 min) are starting assumptions, not validated values.

Illustrative simulation Illustrative

Key finding: the benefit depends on staff logging walk-ins. With live counts and confirmed holds, no simulated group walked to a full station. When 60% of arrivals are walk-ins logged 10 minutes late, groups turned away rise to 112.4 per 100, worse than a 15-minute radio report (101.2). The live count is the product; the station tablet must make logging easy.

Source: engine/simulation_results.json. Assumptions: 5 stations on one line, 50 m/min group pace, group sizes 8–40, stays 20–40 min, mean gap 3.5 min between requests, 300 runs. Synthetic demand only.

Rules, not AI Tested

  1. 1Route approved and open; step-free if the group needs it
  2. 2Station open; data no older than 20 minutes
  3. 3Station offers the services needed
  4. 4Whole group fits: capacity − inside − accepted − pending ≥ group size. Never split.
  5. 5Rank by walking distance, then data freshness

Every suggestion on the coordinator phone has a “Why this station?” checklist, and every station that is not offered shows its reason. Nothing is a black box.

Hard boundaries, and where this prototype enforces them

BoundaryHow the prototype enforces it
Not triage or diagnosisThe emergency instruction is the first thing on the coordinator screen, above every other control.
Never suggest a closed, prohibited or unverified routeRouting uses approved segments only (Scenario 4). Supervisors can close or reopen a segment only with a recorded reason.
Never split a groupRule 4 excludes any station without room for the whole group, and says so (Scenario 2).
Always show data age; never call stale data liveEvery option shows “Live / Ageing / Not live” as text plus minutes. Data older than 20 min is never offered (Scenario 6). Offline mode freezes the list and labels it not live.
No names, medical histories or location trackingInputs are an anonymous count, one of three approximate points and access needs. Nothing else is collected or stored.
Acceptance is not a guaranteed admissionStated on the confirmation card and beside the request button.
Overrides need a recorded reasonThe engine refuses an override without one (Scenario 7); the form stays disabled until a reason is typed.

Proposed

Where ZILL would fit

Existing systems, such as Nusuk and the Ministry of Hajj and Umrah's Monitoring and Control Center, already do a great deal. ZILL is a narrow add-on for one hand-off: from a field team with a tired group to a station team that has confirmed. It replaces nothing, and no integration or partnership exists or is implied.

Next to test

What would change our mind

  • Can station staff keep counts current at peak load? (The simulation says everything depends on it.)
  • Are 3 / 15 / 20 minutes the right limits?
  • Do coordinators trust the “why” explanations enough to act on them?
  • Arabic-first interface and field connectivity.

Data and tools used

  • DataFully synthetic demo area (5 stations, 8 route segments, 1 assembly point) defined in the engine. No real, personal or patient data.
  • Rules enginePython 3 standard library (dataclasses, heapq); shortest path over approved segments.
  • PrototypePlain HTML, CSS, SVG and JavaScript; JavaScript port of the engine; BroadcastChannel for live state across windows. No server, framework, analytics or AI service.
  • Tests7 scripted scenarios (run_scenarios.py), replayed and checked in the browser against the saved Python output.
